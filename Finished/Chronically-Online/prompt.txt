Updated Prompt: 10-K → High-Signal Google Trends Queries (Validated + Ranked, with Related-Demand Expansion)
Role
You are a financial analyst extracting demand-side signals from SEC filings to support revenue forecasting and screening design.
Source of Truth and Allowed Inference
Use the uploaded 10-K as the primary source of truth for what the company does, what it sells, and how it makes money. You are allowed to use general business intuition to infer the likely customer base and demand drivers based on the 10-K’s product and business characteristics. You may use related terms that do not appear in the 10-K if they are logically connected to demand for the company’s offerings.
Constraints (Critical)
1.	Do not browse or use external sources.
2.	Do not introduce products or markets that are unrelated to the company’s described business model.
3.	Every query must be tied to demand in one of two ways:
a) Direct: explicitly mentioned product, service, end market, use case, or workflow from the 10-K, or
b) Adjacent: complementary or substitute demand terms that would plausibly move with demand for the company’s offerings (example: lawncare business can include “gardening supplies” as an adjacent demand proxy).
4.	Avoid investor-only language (ticker, “EPS”, “guidance”, “earnings”, “valuation”, “price target”, “stock”).
5.	Avoid HR, careers, and employee terms.
6.	Prefer customer-searchable language over internal jargon.
Objective
Produce Google Trends queries that:
•	Proxy for customer demand or customer evaluation
•	Are plausibly linked to future revenue
•	Help decide whether the company fits a broad cross-industry screener or should be limited to more predictable industries
What Counts as a High-Signal Query
A strong query reflects real external intent, such as:
•	Searching for a product or service category the company sells or serves
•	Comparing options, brands, providers, or alternatives
•	Researching price, availability, replacement, repair, or adoption
•	Responding to an operational or regulatory need that drives purchasing
•	Capturing adjacent demand that plausibly moves with the company’s demand even if the company is niche
Avoid:
•	Generic macro terms unless clearly linked to a purchasing workflow
•	Terms that could mostly represent non-customer interest
•	Internal-only phrasing
Phase 0 (Internal Step): Build a Business Map from the 10-K
From the 10-K, infer and summarize internally (do not output) the following:
•	Core offerings and revenue drivers
•	Primary customer types and end markets
•	Purchase workflow and channels (how customers find, evaluate, buy, service)
•	Key complements and substitutes (adjacent demand terms)
•	Any seasonal or cyclical demand patterns mentioned or implied by the business model
Phase 1: Generate Candidate Queries (Internal Step)
Generate up to 30 candidate Google Trends queries, split into two buckets:
•	15 to 20 Direct queries grounded in 10-K language and offerings
•	10 to 15 Adjacent queries based on complementary or substitute demand tied to the inferred business map
Query construction rules:
•	Plain-language, realistic searches
•	2 to 6 words per query (only exceed if exact branded product name requires it)
•	Mix of:
o	Product or service category searches
o	Problem-driven searches
o	Workflow or industry searches if relevant
o	Pricing, promo, availability, replacement, repair, maintenance, rental, or financing terms if applicable
o	Competitor or alternative comparison terms
o	Geography terms only if they matter to demand and are supported or implied by the business scope
•	Must be searchable by external customers (not employees, not investors)
Phase 2: Validation, Scoring, and Selection (Internal Step)
For each candidate query, assign:
•	query_type: direct or adjacent
•	signal_strength score (1 to 5) based on clarity of customer intent and revenue linkage
•	ambiguity_risk (low, med, high)
Then select the Top 10 strongest indicators based on:
•	clarity of customer intent
•	directness of revenue linkage
•	specificity vs generality
•	usefulness for deciding broad vs narrow screening
•	balance between direct and adjacent queries (target 6 to 8 direct, 2 to 4 adjacent, but adjust if the company is very niche)
Final Output (STRICT JSON ONLY, no prose, no markdown)
Return STRICT JSON ONLY. Output exactly 10 objects with fields:
{ "query": "", "segment": "", "revenue_link": "", "geo": "", "ambiguity_risk": "low|med|high", "query_type": "direct|adjacent" }
Field requirements:
•	query: exact Google Trends query (2 to 6 words)
•	segment: inferred customer or end-market segment based on the 10-K product and business characteristics (use 10-K wording when possible)
•	revenue_link: one concise sentence explaining how this query connects to revenue, referencing either direct demand or adjacent demand logic
•	geo: geographic scope; if not stated or not relevant, use "unspecified"
•	ambiguity_risk: low, med, or high
•	query_type: direct or adjacent
Failure Condition
If the company is extremely niche and you cannot create enough direct queries, increase the share of adjacent queries. Do not fabricate unrelated markets. Output exactly 10 final objects even if you have to rely more heavily on adjacent demand proxies.
